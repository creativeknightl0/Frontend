The Content is Somthing

Fargo is a city in North Dakota, located on the border with Minnesota, along the Red River of the North. It's known for its fertile farmland in the Red River Valley and for being a central hub in the Fargo-Moorhead metropolitan area. Fargo is also known for the Coen brothers' film "Fargo" (1996), which, while set in the region, is a fictional story loosely inspired by a real-life crime. More about Fargo: Location: Situated in Cass County, North Dakota, on the border with Minnesota. History: Founded in 1871 and named after William Fargo, a founder of Wells Fargo. Metropolitan Area: Part of the larger Fargo-Moorhead metropolitan area, which includes West Fargo. Government: Governed by a part-time mayor and four city commissioners. Fargo (Film): The 1996 movie "Fargo" is a fictional story inspired by a real-life crime, but the events in the movie are not based on actual events. "Fargo" (TV Series): An anthology crime drama series that, while inspired by the movie, features its own self-contained narratives across different seasons and settings, often in Minnesota. Climate: Average high temperatures range from 16°F in January to 82°F in July. Tourism: The Fargo-Moorhead area offers attractions like the Red River Zoo, the historic Fargo Theatre, and various cultural and recreational activities.



20th century


Early in the century, the automobile industry flourished, and in 1905, Fargo became home to the Pence Automobile Company, a company that at one time distributed 29% of all Buicks.[15] In addition, Fargo also hosted a regional Ford assembly plant, which by 1917 was assembling some 70 cars per day. The plant would remain in operation until 1956.[16][17] On Labor Day in 1910, Theodore Roosevelt visited Fargo to lay the cornerstone of the college's new library.[18] To a crowd of 30,000, Roosevelt spoke about his first visit to Fargo 27 years earlier, and credited his experience homesteading in North Dakota for his eventual rise to the presidency.[18] Fargo-Moorhead boomed after World War II, and the city grew rapidly. In 1957, it experienced the 1957 Fargo tornado that destroyed a large part of the north end of the city.[19] Ted Fujita, famous for his Fujita tornado scale, analyzed pictures of the Fargo tornado, helping him develop his ideas for "wall cloud" and "tail cloud." These were the first major scientific descriptive terms associated with tornadoes.[20][21] The construction of two interstates (I-29 and I-94) revolutionized travel in the region and pushed growth of Fargo to the south and west of the city limits. In 1972, the West Acres Shopping Center, the largest shopping mall in North Dakota, was constructed near the intersection of the two Interstates.



Recent


Fargo has continued to expand rapidly but steadily. Since the mid-1980s, the bulk of new residential growth has occurred in the south and southwest zones of the area (for example in West Fargo) due to geographic constraints on the north side.[23] The city's major retail districts on the southwest side have likewise seen rapid development. Downtown Fargo has been gentrified due in part to investments by the city and private developers in the Renaissance Zone.[24] Most older neighborhoods, such as Horace Mann, have either avoided decline or been revitalized through housing rehabilitation promoted by planning agencies to strengthen the city's core. NDSU has grown rapidly into a major research university and forms a major component of the city's identity and economy.[25]
Since the late 1990s, the Fargo-Moorhead Metropolitan Statistical Area has consistently had one of the lowest unemployment rates among MSAs in the United States.[27] On July 14, 2023, 37-year-old Mohamad Barakat opened fire on a group of police officers in the city who were responding to an unrelated traffic accident. One officer was killed and two others were injured before Barakat was killed by one of the officers at the scene.[28]



Demographics



As of the 2022 American Community Survey, there are 56,890 estimated households in Fargo with an average of 2.14 persons per household. The city has a median household income of $64,432. Approximately 13.3% of the city's population lives at or below the poverty line. Fargo has an estimated 74.5% employment rate, with 42.4% of the population holding a bachelor's degree or higher and 95.0% holding a high school diploma.[40] The top nine reported ancestries (people were allowed to report up to two ancestries, thus the figures will generally add to more than 100%) were German (33.2%), Norwegian (21.8%), Irish (8.5%), English (5.1%), Subsaharan African (5.1%), French (except Basque) (3.5%), Italian (1.9%), Polish (1.8%), and Scottish (0.9%). The median age in the city was 31.5 years.
